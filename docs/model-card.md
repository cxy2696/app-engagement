# Model card: EngageGraph ranker

This card covers the four reference models shipped with EngageGraph 2.2.1. It follows the Model Cards for Model Reporting framework (Mitchell et al., 2019). Aggregate metrics are generated by `engagegraph report`; the authoritative values live in `reports/results.json` and the run artefacts.

## 1. Model details

| Model | Family | Input | Output | Fitted parameters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `popularity` | Non-personalised prior | January app support | Global score per app | 9,848 scores |
| `itemknn` | Memory-based CF | January user–app matrix | Score = Σ co-usage similarity | Top-100 neighbours per app |
| `als` | Latent factor CF | January confidence matrix | Dot product of user/item factors | 34,024 × 64 + 9,848 × 64 |
| `metapath2vec` | Heterogeneous graph embedding | January user–app graph | Dot product of embeddings | 43,872 × 64 |

All models are trained on the same January matrix and evaluated on the same February labels. The default primary model for the demo is chosen by NDCG@10 on the benchmark, not by hand. The artefact manifest records the exact model type, hyperparameters, dataset fingerprint, package version, and creation time.

The models are domain-agnostic: `data.domain` supplies vocabulary, task labels, and an optional serving blocklist, while the matrices and objectives stay the same. See [`generality.md`](generality.md) for adapting the engine to products, content, or SaaS features.

### Hyperparameters (primary benchmark)

| Model | Settings |
| --- | --- |
| Popularity | user support, `log1p` |
| Item-kNN | cosine, binary, top 100, block size 512 |
| ALS | 64 factors, λ = 0.05, 15 iterations, α = 40, seed 42 |
| MetaPath2Vec | dim 64, walk 20, context 5, 5 walks/node, 5 negatives, 3 epochs, lr 0.01, seed 42 |

The primary values were fixed before February. MetaPath2Vec's read-out and epoch count were revised after a February diagnostic; see [`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §3.5 for the disclosure.

## 2. Intended use

**Primary:** ranking apps a panelist is likely to adopt next month, for research benchmarks, internal analytics, and recommendation prototypes where the operator has lawful access to comparable panel or first-party telemetry.

**Users:** data scientists, ML engineers, and product analysts who need a reproducible baseline before investing in a bespoke recommender.

**Out of scope:** individual-level targeting without consent, causal claims about why a user switches apps, credit/insurance/employment decisions, content moderation, and any use requiring demographic fairness guarantees. The data contains no demographics, so group fairness cannot be evaluated.

## 3. Training data

January 2024 aggregated panel exports: 34,024 eligible panelists, 9,848 catalogue apps, 2,077,959 interactions. February 2024 provides 561,231 binary adoption labels across 28,286 evaluation users. Full details, provenance gaps, and privacy notes: [`data-card.md`](data-card.md).

## 4. Evaluation

- **Task:** full-catalogue next-month new-app adoption ranking.
- **Protocol:** January fit, February labels; all January apps masked; catalogue fixed from January. Primary configs were fixed before evaluation; post-hoc MetaPath2Vec/item-kNN diagnostics are disclosed in [`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §3.5.
- **Primary metric:** NDCG@10, macro-averaged over 28,286 users.
- **Secondary:** Recall@10/20/50, MAP, HitRate, MRR, coverage, Gini, novelty.
- **Uncertainty:** paired user-level bootstrap (1,000 resamples), 95% intervals; Holm–Bonferroni across comparisons against popularity.
- **Cold start:** 10,568 February users have no January history; 307 have January history but fewer than three catalogue items and are reported as ineligible.

## 5. Explainability

Every served recommendation carries up to two reasons of the form "co-used with *X* in January", plus a within-user percentile (`q`) that locates the item among the user's unseen candidates. `q` is a relative confidence, not a probability; see [`algorithm.md`](algorithm.md).

For item-kNN the reasons are exact neighbour contributions. For ALS and MetaPath2Vec they are post-hoc co-usage explanations and must not be read as latent-factor attributions. Popularity recommendations are explained by January support. Explanations are descriptive, not causal. The serving layer also applies a small actionability blocklist of OS components and measurement agents; the offline benchmark keeps the full catalogue, so the table's metrics are not blocklist-filtered.

## 6. Limitations and risks

1. **Distribution shift.** The model is fitted on one month and evaluated on the next. Performance will degrade under app launches, policy changes, seasonality, or panel composition changes.
2. **Popularity bias.** All collaborative models inherit popularity skew. Coverage and Gini are reported to make this visible; a production system should add exploration or explicit diversity.
3. **Cold start.** New users and new apps have no January signal. The API falls back to popularity; this is simple but weak.
4. **Label noise.** A missing February interaction is treated as non-adoption; panel attrition can look like disinterest.
5. **Weight ambiguity.** The duration unit is inferred. Ranking metrics are scale-invariant, but any business KPI expressed in hours is not validated.
6. **No fairness audit.** Without demographics, disparate impact cannot be measured. Operators must run their own audit on first-party data.
7. **Offline-only evidence.** No online A/B test, no position-bias correction, no counterfactual evaluation. Treat the benchmark as a screening tool, not proof of business lift.

## 7. Ethical considerations

The model ranks apps for pseudonymous panelists. Even without demographics, app usage can reveal sensitive interests. Deployments should minimise retention of raw identifiers, restrict bulk scoring, provide user-facing transparency where required, and avoid using the scores to infer sensitive attributes. The demo is not a public profiling service.

## 8. Maintenance

- Retrain monthly on a rolling window; keep the previous artefact for rollback.
- Compare the new model against the incumbent on a held-out future month, not on the training month.
- Monitor input drift (catalogue size, support distribution, weight quantiles) and output drift (coverage, novelty, score distribution).
- Re-run `engagegraph benchmark` after dependency upgrades; artefacts record the package version and dataset fingerprint.
- Deprecation and review process: [`governance.md`](governance.md), [`operations.md`](operations.md).
